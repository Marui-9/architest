Here’s how to evolve ArchiTest into a product with real business potential:

1) Expand Static Analysis Beyond Contracts

Current ArchiTest leans into API contract testing. That’s useful but niche.

Recommended additions:

Architecture guardrails module:

Unused services

Privilege exposure

Network misconfigurations

Shared volume risks

Coupling/circular dependency flags

Rule library: configurable policies (“fail on public DB exposure”)

Scoring & risk dashboard: quantifies architecture quality

This turns it into a more general architectural quality analyzer — a bigger problem than just contract test mapping.

2) CI/CD & Policy Enforcement

Dev teams care most when tools fit into workflows.

Add:

CI integrations (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI)

PR checks that block “bad” changes

Alerts on drift from baseline architecture

This turns ArchiTest into active governance, not just visualization.

3) Better Diffing and Historical Tracking

Version-to-version comparisons produce tangible value:

“Your complexity score increased”

“New risky exposure introduced”

“Coupling increased by X”

This is where SaaS can charge.

4) Visual Storytelling Layer

Keep the clickable graph, but expand to include:

Highlighted risks

Scoring colors

Hooks into IDEs or Slack

The static graph becomes a decision support and risk communication tool, not just a utility.

Recommended Product Positioning

Position your expanded ArchiTest as:

Architecture guardrails for containerized development teams
(“Understand, score, and enforce safe infrastructure before runtime.”)

This resonates with:

Cloud-native teams

Early stage startups

Mid-sized engineering teams

DevSecOps practitioners

Contrasts well with existing tools:

Not just contract tests

Not purely runtime observability

Not just YAML linters

Pricing Model

Open Source Core

CLI + rules + basic graph export

Free and extensible

Hosted SaaS (Paid)
Tiered pricing examples:

Team: CI check integrations + dashboards

Pro: Risk scoring + team policies + alerts

Enterprise: SSO, audit logs, custom rules

Hosted SaaS adoption makes sense because many teams:

Don’t want to host their own infrastructure

Want integrated tooling + alerts

Prefer a managed console
